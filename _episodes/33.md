description: |
  Deep Dives with Iman, hosted by Iman Mossavat, features Dr. Kushal Kedia, a postdoc at MIT.

  Robot hands have become remarkably good, and Kedia thinks we are wasting most of what they can do. His diagnosis is that hardware has jumped ahead of software. A robot taught by demonstration can only be as good as the person puppeteering it, and a human is a master of their own body, not of a five-fingered hand with 22 joints. He wants "the max juice" out of the hardware, meaning letting the robot find things nobody showed it.

  Play2Perfect, a paper Kedia co-authored with colleagues, starts with play. Before the hand is trained to insert a peg or screw something in, it plays with random objects, a bit like a baby, until it controls its fingers. The precise assembly then takes two to five hours to learn, with no hand-engineered reward.

  Then the uncomfortable part: a robot that beats its teacher will sometimes make a "Move 37", a move that looks like a blunder until you understand it. Kedia wants that, but legible to the people standing next to the robot.
